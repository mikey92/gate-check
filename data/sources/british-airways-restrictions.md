Power bank limits: Up to 2 power banks per person; Power banks must display capacity rating information.; Aircraft seat power supplies are to be used exclusively for charging personal electronic devices (PEDs), not power banks.; Power banks must be stored either in the seat pocket or in a bag placed under the seat in front.; Power banks must not exceed 100Wh. All battery restrictions: Max. 4 spare batteries per person (of which up to 2 may be power banks), kept in original packaging or insulated/protected from contact with metal. Lithium metal batteries must not exceed 2g lithium content and lithium-ion batteries must not exceed 100Wh. ... On British Airways flights, you will need approval for each passenger carrying lithium rechargeable batteries of 100-160Wh or lithium metal batteries containing up to 8g of lithium metal. ... Please note that this form is not for power banks, battery-operated wheelchairs, mobility scooters or mobility aids.

Statements quoted from this page:

- Limit without approval: "Power banks must not exceed 100Wh"
- Limit with approval: "On British Airways flights, you will need approval for each passenger carrying lithium rechargeable batteries of 100-160Wh ... Please note that this form is not for power banks, | Lithium battery/power banks of more than 100Wh up to 160Wh: These must be detached from the bag and protected from accidental activation with terminals insulated/protected from short circuits before being carried into the cabin."
- Number allowed: "Up to 2 power banks per person | Max. 4 spare batteries per person (of which up to 2 may be power banks)"
- Carry-on or checked: "power banks and spare batteries are forbidden in checked baggage and must be carried in your hand baggage. | If your cabin bag is checked in or removed at the gate and placed in the hold, you must remove all spare batteries and power banks and carry them with you in the cabin."
- Recharging it in flight: "Aircraft seat power supplies are to be used exclusively for charging personal electronic devices (PEDs), not power banks."
- Overhead bin: "Power banks must be stored either in the seat pocket or in a bag placed under the seat in front."
- Terminal protection: "Spare batteries must be protected from short circuits and damage by keeping them in their original packaging (if possible), in a protective case or a strong plastic bag, or by placing electrical tape over the terminals and carried in the cabin."
- Labelling: "Power banks must display capacity rating information. | If the watt hour (Wh) rating is not shown on the battery or cannot be determined, then the battery cannot be accepted onboard."
- mAh to Wh: "Milliamp hour rating/1000 multiplied by the voltage equals Wh."
