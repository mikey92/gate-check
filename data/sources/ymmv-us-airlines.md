American Airlines - Policy: Restricted Items - Lithium batteries and power banks must be carried in carry-on baggage. - American permits up to four spare batteries of less than 100 Wh. - Up to two spare batteries between 100 and 160 Wh are permitted. ... Delta Air Lines - Policy: Battery or Fuel-Powered Items - Power banks and other spare lithium batteries must be carried in carry-on baggage. - Batteries of 100 Wh or less are permitted for personal use. - Up to two spare batteries between 100 and 160 Wh are allowed. - Batteries over 160 Wh are prohibited. - Delta limits each passenger to a maximum of 20 spare batteries. ... Southwest Airlines - ... When a power bank is being used, it must remain visible and cannot be stored inside a bag. - A power bank may not be charged onboard using the aircraft’s in-seat power supply. - Southwest otherwise applies the FAA’s limits for battery size, approval and terminal protection.

Statements quoted from this page:

- Limit without approval: "100 watt-hours or less: Generally permitted without advance airline approval when carried for personal use."
- Limit with approval: "More than 100 Wh but no more than 160 Wh: Airline approval is required, with no more than two spare batteries per passenger."
- Using it in flight: "Southwest and Avelo specifically state that a power bank must remain visible while charging another device. ... Other airlines may not publish a separate power-bank usage rule, but the flight crew always has the authority to tell passengers to disconnect or stow an electronic device."
- Overhead bin: "Power banks should remain accessible during the flight rather than being buried inside an overhead bag."
- mAh to Wh: "Watt-hours = milliamp-hours × voltage ÷ 1,000 ... 20,000 × 3.7 ÷ 1,000 = 74 Wh"
