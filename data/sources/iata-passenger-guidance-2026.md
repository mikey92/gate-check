Power banks. Recent events have resulted in further proposed restrictions regarding power banks carried by passengers on aircraft. While it will take some time for these proposals to be accepted into regulation, airlines are moving early to adopt these measures in the interests of aviation safety. ☞ Passengers are limited to a maximum of two lithium-ion power banks not exceeding 100 Wh. ☞ Power banks must not be recharged from an in-aircraft power supply. ☞ Power banks should not be stored in the overhead lockers ☞ Power banks should not be used to provide power to other devices during taxi, take-off or landing. Airlines may impose more stringent conditions on the carriage and use of power banks by passengers. ... Table 1 ... ≤ 100 Wh ≤ 2 g ... Power bank Yes 3 No No 3 ... > 100 Wh but ≤ 160 Wh ... Statements quoted from this page:

- Limit without approval: "Passengers are limited to a maximum of two lithium-ion power banks not exceeding 100 Wh."
- Limit with approval: "Table 1, row '> 100 Wh but ≤ 160 Wh', configuration 'Power bank': 'Forbidden' | 8. Check battery size: For larger batteries (over 100 watt-hours, such as those used in larger cameras, drones, or power tools), check with your airline as approval may be required."
- Number allowed: "Passengers are limited to a maximum of two lithium-ion power banks not exceeding 100 Wh. | 3. Limited to a maximum of 2 power banks. | 2. Each person is limited to a maximum of 20 spare batteries of any type. The operator may approve the carriage of more than 20 batteries."
- Carry-on or checked: "Table 1: 'Power bank' — Carry-on baggage: Yes; Checked baggage: No | Spare batteries must be individually protected ... and carried in carry-on baggage only."
- Using it in flight: "Power banks should not be used to provide power to other devices during taxi, take-off or landing"
- Recharging it in flight: "Power banks must not be recharged from an in-aircraft power supply."
- Overhead bin: "Power banks should not be stored in the overhead lockers | Do not recharge it prior to boarding and then store it in the overhead locker."
- Terminal protection: "6. Protect loose batteries: Keep spare batteries and power banks in their original packaging or cover the terminals with tape to prevent short-circuits."
- Certification: "All batteries which are being caried by passengers or being transported as cargo on a passenger aircraft must be of a type that meets the requirements of the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria, Part III, subsection 38.3."
- Labelling: "All lithium-ion batteries are required to have the Watt-hour rating marked on the outside of the battery case. If passenger handling staff are unable to verify the Watt-hour rating by checking either the battery, or the user documentation, the operator may not permit the carriage of the lithium battery, or the lithium battery powered device."
- mAh to Wh: "Watt-hour rating, expressed in Wh, shows the power of the lithium cell or battery, which is calculated by multiplying the rated capacity in ampere-hours by the nominal voltage (Wh = Ah x V)."
- Effective date: "It is based on the provisions set out in the 2025-2026 Edition of the ICAO Technical Instructions for the Safe Transport of Dangerous Goods by Air (Technical Instructions) and the 67th Edition (2026) of the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR). | Recent events have resulted in further proposed restrictions regarding power banks carried by passengers on aircraft. While it will take some time for these proposals to be accepted into regulation, airlines are moving early to adopt these measures in the interests of aviation safety."
